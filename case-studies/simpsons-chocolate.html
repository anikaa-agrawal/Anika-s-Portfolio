Case study — 02 · Audience & growth strategy

Simpson's Chocolate: the strategy I'd run before spending a single rupee

I picked Simpson's Chocolate as a live strategy exercise — a real Lucknow home bakery with genuinely good products and a 4.7-star reputation, but no paid distribution and no website. My rule for myself: no Ads Manager, no targeting, no creative brief until I actually understood who I was talking to and why. This is that groundwork.

Meta Ads Audience Strategy Customer Journey Mapping D2C / Home Bakery
01

What I was working with

Simpson's Chocolate is Anushka's business — flavoured chocolates and gift hampers, made and packed from a home bakery in Lucknow. It didn't start as a business at all; it started as her handing out homemade chocolates to friends and family, who kept telling her they were good enough to sell. Eventually she believed them, invested in proper moulds, and built out a real range: mango, vanilla, milkbar, dates, and festive hampers with juices, snacks, and personalised wish cards.

Location
Lucknow, B2C
Product range
Chocolate boxes ₹400–900 · gift hampers ₹700–1,500
Revenue at start
~₹10K/month (~₹1.2L/year)
Reputation
4.7★ across 181 Google reviews
02

Where the actual gap was

The product wasn't the problem — a 4.7-star rating on 181 reviews tells you the chocolate is genuinely good. What stood out to me was that every single order still arrived the same way: a DM, from someone who already knew Anushka or knew someone who did. She'd done the right early moves — listed the business on Google Maps, stayed consistent on Instagram and Facebook — but there was no website and no paid distribution behind any of it, so the audience never grew past friends, family, and word of mouth.

That's the gap I chose to solve for: not "make more content," but build the audience logic that could actually turn a stranger scrolling past an ad into someone placing an order — right as festive season, four new chocolate flavours, and new gift hampers were all landing at the same time.

03

The goal I set — and how I'd know it's working

SMART goal

Grow revenue from chocolate boxes and gift hampers by 30% within 6 months of the festive season, using the four newly launched flavours and the new hamper line as the entry point.

  • 01

    Revenue / sales

    The number that actually matters — overall revenue growth tracked against the 30% target, driven by the new launches.

  • 02

    Inbound DMs

    Since every order still starts as a DM, I'm tracking volume of inbound inquiries across every platform she takes orders on.

  • 03

    Engagement

    A read on whether reach is actually moving past friends and family, or just staying flat inside the same circle.

  • 04

    DM-to-conversion rate

    Of the people who message, how many actually order — this tells me if the sales chat itself needs work, not just the ads.

04

Who I'd build this for

I didn't want one broad, vague audience. I split it into two: a primary audience built from scratch on demographics and intent, and a warm audience of people who've already engaged with the Facebook and Instagram pages — so retargeting can carry more of the budget once campaigns are actually live.

Demographics

Men & women, 24–45, within Lucknow + a 10–15km radius. Corporate employees and small business owners with household income of ₹20–25K and up. New parents with kids aged 3–10 who want homemade, chemical-free options.

Interests I'd target
  • Occasional chocolate buyers
  • Homemade / chemical-free food
  • Bakery lovers
  • Lifestyle & shopping enthusiasts
  • Parents of young kids
Behaviours I'd target
  • Festive & occasion-based shopping
  • Corporate gift hamper buying
  • Bulk / gift hamper purchases
  • Keeps chocolate stocked at home
05

How I mapped the customer journey

Before writing a single ad, I forced myself to answer one question at every stage: what would actually move this specific person forward, right now?

Awareness

When is this audience actually paying attention?

Around festival windows — Rakhi, Diwali — when gifting is already on their mind and hampers are genuinely relevant, not an interruption.

Interest

Why would they care about this specifically?

Homemade, hygienic chocolate is a real need for anyone wary of what's on store shelves — and for gift-hunters who want something easy and within budget, this is a straightforward answer.

Desire

How do I show them it fits their actual life?

New parents who want homemade but don't have time to bake, and anyone stuck on what to gift — these are the two groups I'd speak to directly, because the product already fits what they need.

Conversion

What gets them to actually order?

New, customisable flavours paired with a clear discount or a buy-one-get-a-gift offer — something that gives them a reason to order today instead of bookmarking it for later.

Advocacy

How do I turn a buyer into someone who brings the next one?

Packaging that feels premium, a genuine feedback ask after delivery, asking customers what flavour they'd want next, and a referral discount that gives happy customers an actual reason to talk about it.
